📖 42 Evaluator Defense Q&A Cheat Sheet (Click to Expand)
Q1: Why did you use Jacobsthal numbers instead of powers of 2?
Jacobsthal numbers [1, 3, 5, 11, 21...] optimize binary search efficiency. By inserting element b_k when the main chain length is strictly bounded to 2p - 1, every binary search hits the theoretical minimum depth without wasting a comparison.
Q2: Why does the first loser (b₁) take 0 comparisons?
Because b₁ < a₁ by construction, and a₁ is the smallest element of all sorted winners. Therefore, b₁ is guaranteed to be smaller than the entire chain and is inserted at index 0 for free.
Q3: Why is std::vector faster than std::deque in wall-clock time?
std::vector uses a single contiguous block of memory, yielding superior CPU cache locality during binary search. std::deque allocates multiple non-contiguous page chunks linked by a map pointer, causing more cache misses.
Q4: Why bound the binary search to each loser's paired winner?
Every loser b_i was already compared against its winner a_i during Step 1 (b_i < a_i). Thus, b_i can never appear after a_i in the sorted array, cutting the binary search space in half.
